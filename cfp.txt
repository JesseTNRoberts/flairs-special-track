THE CALL
----------------------------------------------------------------------

Deliberative AI, typically in the form of state-space search, proceeds
through recursive generate-and-test cycles: candidates are proposed,
evaluated, pruned, and refined in pursuit of a goal or more general
utilitarian ideal. Generative AI,
epitomized by transformer-based large language models, is instead
characterized by a weak or trivial test step, producing outputs by
sampling from learned conditional probability distributions.

A growing body of work treats these paradigms as powerful and necessary
complements. Search can structure and constrain LLM generation, and LLMs
can serve as flexible components within a broader search process: as
generators of successors, as scorers of states, and as tie-breakers.
Very often, deliberative, Type 2 methods are exploited during machine
learning to push knowledge from the test phase increasingly into the
generate phase, which results in powerful generative-dominant systems
when learning is over massive data. At the same time, an important,
inverse question remains largely open: how, if at all, does
deliberative, Type 2 behavior emerge from a fundamentally generative,
Type 1 system?

Following "State-Space Search and Transformer-Based AI Hybridizations"
(Fisher, Moore, and Roberts; available on the track page) and an invited
talk by Vaishak Belle, both at ACS 2026, as well as the foundational
research these works draw on, this special track invites papers that
explore, build, evaluate, or theorize about hybrid architectures that
combine deliberative and transformer-based generative AI. It equally
invites papers on foundational questions of how Type 1 and Type 2
processing relate to and give rise to one another. We welcome systems
papers, empirical studies, and position or theoretical contributions
addressing questions such as:

  - How can LLMs serve as generators or evaluators within classical
    search algorithms?
  - How can search principles improve or control LLM decoding?
  - What is the theoretical relationship between LLM inference and
    generic state-space search?
  - By what mechanisms might a generative system come to behave
    deliberatively?

----------------------------------------------------------------------
TOPICS (including, but not limited to)
----------------------------------------------------------------------

  - Proposer-verifier, generate-and-test, and LLM-modulo architectures
    pairing LLMs with symbolic checkers, solvers, or simulators
  - LLM-guided search for planning, routing, program synthesis, and
    mathematical proof
  - Search-structured decoding: beam search, lookahead, and Monte
    Carlo-style rollouts applied to LLM generation
  - Scoring and filtering of generated candidates, including
    self-assessment, auxiliary discriminative models, and tool-based
    scoring
  - Reinforcement learning that folds evaluative, test-like signal back
    into generative models
  - Retrieval- and generation-augmented reasoning (RAG/GAR)
  - Neurosymbolic integration, e.g. concept nets or commonsense graphs
    paired with generative reasoning
  - Evidence for latent planning or lookahead in transformer
    representations
  - Theoretical and empirical characterizations of Type 1 and Type 2
    processing, and of how Type 2 might emerge from Type 1
  - Computational cost and efficiency trade-offs in hybrid systems
  - Applications in mathematics, transportation and robotics planning,
    scholarly and bibliographic reasoning, and interactive narrative
